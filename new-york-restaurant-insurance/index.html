Deli insurance in New York

Delis, bodegas, and sandwich shops in New York usually need the same core coverage as a restaurant.

  • General liability for injury or property damage claims at the shop
  • Property coverage for the space, improvements, and equipment such as cases, slicers, and refrigeration
  • Equipment breakdown coverage when it is added, which is separate from property coverage
  • Spoilage and food contamination coverage when added, each with its own terms and limits
  • Workers compensation if you have employees, including part-time staff
  • New York disability benefits and paid family leave, which are separate from workers compensation
  • Liquor liability if you sell beer or other alcohol, because a general liability policy often limits or excludes it
  • Hired and non-owned auto if you or staff use a personal car for deliveries or pickups, which is not on every policy by default

What a New York landlord lease often asks for

A lease often asks for general liability with the landlord named as additional insured, property coverage on your improvements and equipment, and a certificate that matches the lease language.

What insurance does a restaurant need in New York City?

Most New York City restaurants carry general liability and property coverage, often on a businessowners policy, plus workers compensation and disability benefits if they have employees. A lease and, if you have sidewalk seating, city rules can set added requirements. Coverage is subject to underwriting and the issued policy.

What insurance does a restaurant or deli in Queens or Brooklyn need?

The same core coverages as elsewhere in New York: a businessowners policy or a package that can include general liability and property, plus workers compensation and disability benefits if you have employees. A lease may ask for higher limits and additional insured status. If you sell alcohol, liquor liability can also apply, subject to the policy and the license. Send the lease and the current policy for a review.

Do restaurants in Brooklyn or the rest of New York City need workers compensation?

New York generally requires workers compensation once you have employees, including part-time help. Disability benefits and Paid Family Leave can also apply. The New York State Workers' Compensation Board explains when coverage is required. If you have no employees and may be exempt, that is a Board determination, not something we can promise.

Do restaurants need workers comp in New York?

Yes, if the restaurant has employees, including part-time staff. New York also requires disability benefits for employers. A businessowners policy does not include either one. The Workers' Compensation Board explains workers compensation coverage requirements and disability benefits coverage. If you have no employees, ask about an exemption before you assume you are clear.

Is food spoilage covered?

Only when the policy includes spoilage coverage, usually with its own limit. It is meant for food that spoils after a covered breakdown or a power loss, depending on the form. It is not on every restaurant policy by default. Ask to see the spoilage limit on the declarations page.

What insurance does Dining Out NYC require for sidewalk or roadway seating?

Dining Out NYC asks for general liability of $1 million per occurrence and $2 million aggregate, naming the City of New York, plus workers compensation and disability benefits. Other items can apply based on how the restaurant operates. Check the Dining Out NYC insurance requirements before you file. The program can change.

How much does restaurant insurance cost?

It depends on sales, payroll, the kitchen, the building, and past claims. We do not publish a price. Send the current policy, or the insurance section of the lease, and we will quote from the facts of that restaurant.

What insurance does a New York deli or sandwich shop need?

A deli, bodega, or sandwich shop usually needs general liability and property coverage, often as a businessowners policy, plus workers compensation, New York disability benefits, and paid family leave if it has employees. Spoilage, food contamination, liquor liability, and hired and non-owned auto are added when the operation or the lease calls for them. What is covered depends on the policy.

If my deli sells beer, do I need liquor liability?

If you sell beer or other alcohol, ask about liquor liability. General liability forms often limit or exclude claims that arise from serving or selling alcohol. Many leases ask for liquor liability when alcohol is sold. There is no general statute that requires every licensee to buy it. Whether a claim is covered depends on the policy.

Does deli insurance cover deliveries in a personal car?

Not automatically. If you or your staff use a personal car for deliveries or pickups, ask about hired and non-owned auto. It is a separate coverage and is not on every policy by default. A vehicle the business owns needs commercial auto, which is different. What is covered depends on the policy.

What insurance does a New York landlord usually ask a deli to carry?

A lease often asks for general liability with the landlord as additional insured, property coverage on your improvements and equipment, and a certificate that matches the lease wording. Many leases also ask for an umbrella, a waiver of subrogation, and liquor liability if you sell alcohol. Send the insurance section of the lease with your quote request.
