Common questions

What insurance does a restaurant need in New York City?

A restaurant, cafe, or deli in New York City usually carries property coverage and general liability, often packaged as a businessowners policy, plus an umbrella when the lease asks. If you have employees, New York requires workers compensation and disability benefits, which are separate policies. The Workers' Compensation Board explains workers compensation coverage requirements. A lease can ask for more. The same parts apply on Long Island.

Do restaurants need workers comp in New York?

Yes, if the restaurant has employees, including part-time staff. New York also requires disability benefits for employers. A businessowners policy does not include either one. The Workers' Compensation Board explains workers compensation coverage requirements and disability benefits coverage. If you have no employees, ask about an exemption before you assume you are clear.

Is food spoilage covered?

Only when the policy includes spoilage coverage, usually with its own limit. It is meant for food that spoils after a covered breakdown or a power loss, depending on the form. It is not on every restaurant policy by default. Ask to see the spoilage limit on the declarations page.

What insurance does Dining Out NYC require for sidewalk or roadway seating?

Dining Out NYC asks for general liability of $1 million per occurrence and $2 million aggregate, naming the City of New York, plus workers compensation and disability benefits. Other items can apply based on how the restaurant operates. Check the Dining Out NYC insurance requirements before you file. The program can change.

How much does restaurant insurance cost?

It depends on sales, payroll, the kitchen, the building, and past claims. We do not publish a price. Send the current policy, or the insurance section of the lease, and we will quote from the facts of that restaurant.
