What is the difference between loss of rents and unpaid rent?

Loss of rents is income the building loses because a covered property loss, such as a fire, makes the space unusable while it is repaired. Unpaid rent is money a tenant owes and does not pay while the apartment is intact. A building policy can cover the first, up to its limit. It does not cover the second. The issued policy governs.

Does landlord insurance cover loss of rent?

It can pay loss of rents while a covered loss, such as a fire, is repaired. It does not pay unpaid rent when a tenant stops paying, leaves without paying, or is evicted for nonpayment. It pays only up to the limit, and only for the time on the policy. The issued policy governs.

What is loss of rents coverage?

Loss of rents is the rent you lose while covered damage is repaired. It is part of many building policies, with its own limit. It is not coverage for empty units or unpaid rent. Read the declarations page for the limit and how long it lasts.

Does landlord insurance cover tenant damage?

It can cover sudden damage to the building that the policy lists as covered. It does not cover wear and tear or slow maintenance problems. It also does not cover a tenant's furniture or other belongings. Tenants need their own renters policy for their property.

Does landlord insurance cover a tenant's injury?

Premises liability on a landlord or building policy can cover injury to a tenant or a visitor when you are responsible for it. It does not cover every injury, and it does not replace health insurance. What is paid depends on the facts and on the policy. The issued policy governs.

What is ordinance or law coverage, and why does an older building need it?

After a covered loss, an older New York City building may have to be repaired to the current building code. That work can cost more than putting back what was there. Ordinance or law coverage can pay some of that extra cost, up to its own limit, when the policy includes it. Not every building policy includes it, so ask to see the limit.

Does landlord insurance cover a tenant's belongings?

No. The building policy covers the building, not a tenant's furniture, clothes, or other property. A tenant needs a renters policy for those things. Your policy and theirs are separate.

Landlord vs homeowners insurance: what is the difference?

A homeowners policy is for a home you live in. A rental needs a landlord or building policy, even if you once lived there. Tell the insurer the building is rented. The wrong policy can leave a rental uncovered.

My building insurance was non-renewed. What now?

Send the non-renewal notice and the loss runs, and tell the lender before the policy ends. Start before the expiration date so there is time to place a new policy. We can review what you have and what other insurers will write. Call (516) 226-7969.
