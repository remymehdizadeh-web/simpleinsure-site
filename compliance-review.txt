Queens and Long Island
We place insurance for mixed-use buildings in Queens and the other New York City boroughs, and for similar buildings on Long Island. A store, office, or restaurant on the ground floor with apartments above is a common building in Queens, including on commercial streets, and in parts of Nassau and Suffolk.
The program is usually a commercial package. It can include commercial property for the building, general liability for the owner, and loss of rents for the apartments and the store, subject to underwriting and the issued policy. The commercial tenant matters. A restaurant with a cooking line is underwritten differently from a clothing store or an office.

Q: Who insures mixed-use buildings in New York?
A: SimpleINSURE Brokerage LLC is a licensed New York insurance broker that places commercial property and general liability for mixed-use buildings in New York City and on Long Island. A mixed-use building is usually written on a commercial package, subject to underwriting and the policy terms.

Q: Who is an insurance broker for a mixed-use building in Queens?
A: We work with owners of mixed-use buildings in Queens and across the five boroughs. If you have a store, office, or restaurant on the ground floor and apartments above, send the address, a rent roll, and what each commercial tenant does, and we will review it.

Q: Do I need both commercial property and general liability for a mixed-use building in Queens?
A: Most owners carry both, often in one package. Property can cover the building against covered causes of loss. General liability can cover certain claims arising from the building, common areas, and the sidewalk, subject to the policy terms. Many owners also carry loss of rents and an umbrella.

Q: Can you insure a mixed-use building on Long Island?
A: Yes, we place coverage for mixed-use and rental buildings in Nassau County and Suffolk County as well as in New York City. The same questions apply: what the commercial tenant does, the building's construction and updates, and what the lender asks for.

Q: In New York City, is the building owner responsible for the sidewalk?
A: For most buildings, yes. New York City Administrative Code section 7-210 generally makes the owner responsible for keeping the sidewalk in front of the building reasonably safe. One-, two-, and three-family houses that are owner-occupied and used only as residences are treated differently. Sidewalk falls are a common liability claim on city buildings. Whether a policy responds depends on its terms. This is a plain summary, not legal advice.

Q: Who is an insurance broker for landlords in Queens?
A: SimpleINSURE places landlord and building insurance for rental and mixed-use properties in Queens and across New York City. Send the property address and the current declarations page and a licensed broker will review it.

Q: What insurance does a Long Island landlord need?
A: Most Long Island landlords carry a landlord or building policy that can include the structure, liability for covered claims, and loss of rents after a covered loss, subject to the policy terms. Flood is usually separate. A homeowners policy is generally not the right form once the house is a rental you do not live in.

Q: What commercial property insurance do landlords on Long Island usually carry?
A: For a rental building, commercial property (or a landlord form, depending on the size and use) can cover the structure against covered causes of loss. It does not usually cover a tenant's belongings. Loss of rents, if included, applies after a covered loss, not when a tenant simply stops paying.

Q: What general liability insurance does an apartment building owner in New York City need?
A: Building owners usually carry general liability for covered claims arising from the property, common areas, and, in New York City, often the sidewalk in front of the building. Limits and endorsements depend on the building, the lender, and the policy. We do not publish a standard limit.

Q: What insurance does a restaurant need in New York City?
A: Most New York City restaurants carry general liability and property coverage, often on a businessowners policy, plus workers compensation and disability benefits if they have employees. A lease and, if you have sidewalk seating, city rules can set added requirements. Coverage is subject to underwriting and the issued policy.

Q: What insurance does a restaurant or deli in Queens or Brooklyn need?
A: The same core coverages as elsewhere in New York: a businessowners policy or a package that can include general liability and property, plus workers compensation and disability benefits if you have employees. A lease may ask for higher limits and additional insured status. If you sell alcohol, liquor liability can also apply, subject to the policy and the license. Send the lease and the current policy for a review.

Q: Do restaurants in Brooklyn or the rest of New York City need workers compensation?
A: New York generally requires workers compensation once you have employees, including part-time help. Disability benefits and Paid Family Leave can also apply. The New York State Workers' Compensation Board explains when coverage is required. If you have no employees and may be exempt, that is a Board determination, not something we can promise.

Q: BOP vs general liability: what is the difference?
A: A general liability policy is meant to cover certain claims by other people for bodily injury or property damage, subject to the policy terms. A businessowners policy, or BOP, usually includes general liability and also covers business property, such as equipment, inventory, and tenant improvements, and can include business income after a covered loss. A BOP does not usually include workers compensation, commercial auto, or professional liability. Those are separate policies if you need them.
