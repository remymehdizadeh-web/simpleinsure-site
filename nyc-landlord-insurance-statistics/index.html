NYC Landlord & Building Insurance Statistics, 2026

A reference page for New York City landlords and building owners, and for owners on Long Island who want the city figures beside their own building. Each line names its source and year.

Nothing on this page is a quote, a promise, or a prediction of what a policy will cost. If a number is not here, we did not verify it. For a reading of your own policy, see landlord insurance or apartment and building insurance.

How many New York City rental homes are in small buildings?

The 2023 New York City Housing and Vacancy Survey estimated 87,390 renter-occupied single-family homes. The same table estimated 465,800 occupied single-family homes in all, of which 378,400 were owner-occupied. These are occupied units that were not in a condo or co-op. They are not a count of buildings. (NYC HPD, 2023 NYCHVS Selected Initial Findings, Table 2)

The same table estimated 448,000 occupied units in two-unit buildings, of which 231,600 were renter-occupied, and 317,200 occupied units in buildings of three to five units, of which 256,900 were renter-occupied. A two-unit building can contribute two occupied units, so these are not building counts. (NYC HPD, 2023 NYCHVS Selected Initial Findings, Table 2)

The survey does not publish a separate count of rental buildings with 1 to 4 units. Our own lot count, which includes owner-occupied houses, is in the original-data section below.

How have property insurance costs changed for New York City buildings?

The NYC Rent Guidelines Board measured a 10.5 percent rise in the Insurance Costs part of its Price Index of Operating Costs for buildings that contain rent-stabilized apartments, for the 2025 to 2026 index year. Insurance was 9.5 percent of that index. The prior year's index had risen 18.7 percent. This is a cost index for a market basket of owner expenses. It is not a quote, and it is not what your building will pay. (NYC Rent Guidelines Board, 2026 Price Index of Operating Costs, April 9, 2026)

Over the five years ending in that 2026 index, Insurance Costs rose a cumulative 99.9 percent, the fastest of the seven components. Its weight in the index moved from 6.5 percent in 2022 to 9.5 percent in 2026. (NYC Rent Guidelines Board, 2026 Price Index of Operating Costs)

In the same index, insurance costs rose 11.6 percent for buildings built before 1974 and 5.4 percent for buildings built in 1974 or later. (NYC Rent Guidelines Board, 2026 Price Index of Operating Costs)

For owner-occupied houses of 1 to 4 families, the NAIC average homeowners premium in New York was $1,628 in 2022. The table ranks New York 18th. The U.S. average on the same table was $1,569. The figure is the HO-3 policy, premiums divided by insured house-years. A landlord or building policy is a different form. This is not a price for a rental. (Insurance Information Institute, citing the NAIC homeowners report of May 2025, data year 2022)

On that same table, the average New York renters premium (HO-4, for a tenant's own property) was $169 in 2022. That is not a landlord policy. (Insurance Information Institute, citing NAIC, data year 2022)

How many housing violations and fires did New York City record?

NYC HPD issued 878,481 housing maintenance code violations in Fiscal 2025 (July 2024 through June 2025). That was 217,595 Class A (non-hazardous), 372,811 Class B (hazardous), and 288,075 Class C (immediately hazardous). (NYC Mayor's Management Report, Department of Housing Preservation and Development, Fiscal 2025)

In calendar year 2025, HPD issued 490,138 Class B and Class C violations to buildings that contain rent-stabilized units, down from 533,581 in 2024. The report describes that change as an 8 percent decrease. (NYC Rent Guidelines Board, 2026 Housing Supply Report, May 21, 2026)

As of January 2026, HPD had 71,947 buildings with at least six residential units on its registration file, including 43,378 with rent-stabilized units. (NYC Rent Guidelines Board, 2026 Housing Supply Report)

The Department of Buildings issued 39,901 DOB violations in Fiscal 2025, down from 172,567 in Fiscal 2024. The Mayor's Management Report says the drop is because roughly 124,000 of the Fiscal 2024 violations were for failure to file or correct elevator inspection reports. DOB also issued 52,891 Office of Administrative Trials and Hearings violations in Fiscal 2025. (NYC Mayor's Management Report, Department of Buildings, Fiscal 2025)

FDNY reported 20,180 structural residential fires in Fiscal 2025, up from 19,544 in Fiscal 2024, and 4,887 structural non-residential fires. It reported 2,175 serious fires (alarm level All Hands and above) and 76 civilian fire fatalities. FDNY also issued 47,522 violation orders. (NYC Mayor's Management Report, Fire Department, Fiscal 2025)

What does New York Labor Law 240 mean for a building owner?

New York Labor Law section 240(1) requires contractors and owners, and their agents, to furnish scaffolding, hoists, ladders, and other devices that give proper protection during erection, demolition, repair, alteration, painting, cleaning, or pointing. Owners of one- and two-family dwellings who contract for the work but do not direct or control it are excepted. People often call this the scaffold law. (New York Labor Law § 240, New York Senate compilation, revision shown as September 22, 2014)

The New York Court of Appeals has used the words strict liability and absolute liability for section 240(1). It has also said those words apply only when the statute was violated and the violation was a cause of the injury. If the worker's own conduct was the sole cause, there is no liability under that section. This is a description of the opinion, not a prediction about any claim. (Blake v. Neighborhood Hous. Servs. of N.Y. City, 1 N.Y.3d 280 (2003))

How many New York City buildings are in a flood zone, and how many have NFIP flood insurance?

NYC City Planning counted 80,907 buildings in the 1 percent annual chance floodplain, and 58,927 built tax lots in that floodplain not counting parks. The count is in the March 5, 2021 final environmental impact statement for Zoning for Coastal Flood Resiliency. It uses FEMA's 2007 Flood Insurance Rate Maps and the 2015 preliminary maps together. It is not a 2026 remap. (NYC Department of City Planning, Zoning for Coastal Flood Resiliency FEIS, Chapter 1, Table 1-1, Notice of Completion March 5, 2021)

FEMA's OpenFEMA policy file, as of September 8, 2026, shows 32,273 National Flood Insurance Program contracts in force for the City of New York (community 360497). Those contracts covered 62,421 insured units. A condo association contract can cover more than one unit, and the unit total is FEMA's policyCount field added up. We checked that no contract with an earlier start date was still open on that day. (OpenFEMA NfipPolicies v3, pulled November 10, 2026. Aggregate rows: CSV. Method: script.)

On that same snapshot, 17,504 insured units were occupancy code 11 (single-family, in FEMA's data dictionary) and 7,057 were occupancy code 12 (a residential building with 2, 3, or 4 units, not a condo, insured for all units). 7,497 insured units were flagged as a rental property on the policy record. Records without that flag are not counted here as owner-occupied. (OpenFEMA NfipPolicies v3, as of September 8, 2026)

Of the insured units in force, 16,706 had a current flood zone code starting with A or V. Another 28,054 insured units had no current flood zone on the record, so the zone figure does not cover every contract. (OpenFEMA NfipPolicies v3, as of September 8, 2026)

How many rent-stabilized apartments are in New York City?

NYC HPD estimated 996,600 rent-stabilized units in 2023. That was 27 percent of the housing stock and 41 percent of rental units. About 758,700 of them (about 76 percent) were stabilized under the Emergency Tenant Protection Act. The same report estimated 24,020 rent-controlled units and 1,139,000 market-rate rental units. The city had an estimated 3,705,000 housing units. (NYC HPD, 2023 NYCHVS Selected Initial Findings)

The same survey estimated a citywide net rental vacancy rate of 1.41 percent, with 33,210 units available for rent, out of 2,357,000 occupied and vacant-available rental units. The rent-stabilized vacancy rate was 0.98 percent. The survey period was January through the middle of June 2023. (NYC HPD, 2023 NYCHVS Selected Initial Findings)

The Rent Guidelines Board reported that in 2025 the rent-stabilized stock had an approximate net gain of 21,281 units. At least 11,464 units left stabilization, and about 32,745 entered. This page does not add that change to the 2023 survey count. They are different measurements. (NYC Rent Guidelines Board, Changes to the Rent Stabilized Housing Stock in NYC in 2025, May 21, 2026)

SimpleINSURE original data

The 2026 SimpleINSURE NYC Small Building Index

The index uses NYC Department of City Planning PLUTO release 26v2, the file on NYC Open Data when we pulled it on November 10, 2026. Open Data marked the rows updated on August 24, 2026, and the file had 858,284 tax lots, all version 26v2. A lot is in the index when it has exactly one building and 1 to 4 residential units, and its lot number is outside 1001 to 6999, so a condominium apartment is not counted as its own building. The percentage uses the same building and lot-number rules for every lot with at least one residential unit.

The index is 486,364 tax lots. That is 87.9 percent of the 553,043 one-building lots with at least one residential unit. Of the index lots, 207,501 have 1 residential unit, 197,716 have 2, 62,640 have 3, and 18,507 have 4. These lots include houses the owner lives in. PLUTO does not say whether a unit is rented. (NYC Open Data, Primary Land Use Tax Lot Output (PLUTO), 26v2. Aggregate rows: CSV. Method: script.)
