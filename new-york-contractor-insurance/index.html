Contractor and builders risk insurance in New York

Who buys builders risk, what a permit asks for, and Labor Law 240 in plain words.

Who pays for builders risk insurance, the owner or the contractor?

The construction contract decides who buys builders risk, who is named on it, and who pays the deductible. On many jobs the owner buys the policy, and the general contractor and subcontractors are named for the work they put in place. The parties can agree instead that the general contractor buys the policy and names the owner. Subcontractors still insure their own tools and equipment. A public primer on these interests is available from the International Risk Management Institute.

What insurance does a general contractor need in New York?

A general contractor in New York usually carries general liability, an umbrella, and builders risk when the contract calls for it. If you have employees, New York requires workers compensation and disability benefits. If the company has trucks or cars for the work, commercial auto is a separate policy. For many New York City Buildings permits, licensed contractors also need general liability of at least $1 million per occurrence, kept current.

What is the difference between an additional insured and a certificate holder?

A certificate holder gets a certificate that shows a policy is in force. Being the certificate holder does not add that person to the policy. An additional insured is named on the liability policy, so a covered claim against them can be handled under that policy. Many contracts ask for both, so send the insurance clause and we will match the wording.

Why does Labor Law 240 matter for contractor insurance?

New York Labor Law section 240, often called the scaffold law, requires owners and contractors to provide proper protection for workers using scaffolds, hoists, ladders, and similar devices. The statute names erection, demolition, repair, alteration, painting, cleaning, and pointing of a building or structure. Owners of one- and two-family homes who hire the work and do not direct it are treated differently. People often use the statute for injuries from a fall or from a falling object. Whether it applies depends on the facts. This is a plain summary, not legal advice. The text is New York Labor Law 240. A liability policy may cover these claims, limit them, or leave them out. Read the form before you rely on it.

What is scaffold law insurance in New York?

There is no separate policy named scaffold law insurance. Contractors usually carry general liability and an umbrella, plus workers compensation and disability benefits when they have employees. Whether those liability forms cover a Labor Law 240 claim depends on the policy. Send the forms. For many New York City Buildings permits, a licensed contractor needs general liability of at least $1 million per occurrence, and current workers compensation and disability insurance. NYC Buildings explains that on its contractor permit and insurance page.

What is an action over claim on a New York contractor policy?

Workers compensation usually pays an injured employee's medical care and part of lost wages, and it limits most direct lawsuits against that employer. The worker may still sue the building owner or another contractor. Those parties may then ask the employer to pay them back. That second claim is often called an action over. A general liability policy may cover an action over claim, limit it, or exclude it. Read the forms. The Workers' Compensation Board describes what workers compensation insurance does.

What does NYC DOB need on a certificate of insurance?

For many permits, New York City Buildings asks licensed contractors for general liability of at least $1 million per occurrence, plus current workers compensation and disability benefits. If there are no employees, the City accepts a waiver instead of those two. The certificate has to show policies that are active. NYC Buildings explains this on its contractor permit and insurance page.

What does builders risk cover?

Builders risk covers the work in place and the materials on the job, against causes such as fire, theft, and wind, up to the policy limit. Some causes of loss have a lower limit than the main policy limit. It covers the project property, not injuries.

Does builders risk cover injuries or liability?

No. Builders risk covers the project property, not injuries or liability. Injured workers belong on workers compensation, and injury to other people belongs on general liability. Keep those policies beside the builders risk.

Do I need builders risk for a renovation?

Often yes, when a lender or the contract asks for it. A building policy may not cover work underway or materials stored for the job. Compare the two policies before demolition starts so there is no gap.

When does builders risk end?

It usually ends at completion, at occupancy, or on the date the policy names. Put the permanent building policy in force before that date. Otherwise the building can sit uncovered between the two policies.

What does Nassau County want on a contractor's certificate of insurance?

Nassau County Consumer Affairs wants a current certificate, the insured name as it appears on the license application, and a description of the actual trade. It does not accept the words general contracting, remodeling, carpentry, or home improvement in place of that description. A home improvement license shows bodily injury of $250,000/$500,000 and property damage of $100,000. The certificate holder is Nassau County Department of Consumer Affairs, 240 Old County Road, 3rd Floor, Mineola, New York 11501. If you subcontract, the county also requires liability insurance for subcontracting. Other license types have different limits. The full sheet is on the Nassau County insurance requirements page.

Do contractors need workers comp and disability benefits to get a permit?

Permit offices usually want proof of workers compensation and disability benefits, or an approved exemption, before they issue a permit. New York City Buildings says the same for many contractor permits, with a waiver when there are no employees. Towns on Long Island use New York State forms. The state explains workers compensation coverage requirements and disability benefits coverage. Check the building department for the place where the job is.

Nassau County limits

  • Home improvement license: bodily injury $250,000/$500,000, property damage $100,000.
  • Environmental remediation: bodily injury $1,000,000/$2,000,000, property damage $1,000,000/$2,000,000.
  • Other licenses: bodily injury $100,000/$300,000, property damage $50,000/$50,000, combined $300,000 minimum.
  • Certificate holder: Nassau County Department of Consumer Affairs, 240 Old County Road, 3rd Floor, Mineola, New York 11501.
  • If you subcontract the work, you also need liability insurance for that subcontracting.
  • For all other business licenses, deductibles are not acceptable.
  • The county asks for notice 15 days before a cancellation, non-renewal, or coverage change.
